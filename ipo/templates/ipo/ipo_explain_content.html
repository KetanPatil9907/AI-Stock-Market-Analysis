What is an IPO?

An Initial Public Offering (IPO) is the first time a private company sells its shares to the general public on a stock exchange such as the NSE or BSE. After the IPO, the company becomes publicly traded and anyone can buy or sell its shares.

Why do companies launch an IPO?

1

Raise capital

Money to expand operations, repay debt, or fund new projects.

2

Early investors exit

Founders, employees, and angel or venture investors can sell part of their stake.

3

Credibility

Being listed adds transparency, visibility, and public awareness.

4

Future funding

Listed shares can be used to raise more capital later and to fund acquisitions.

How an IPO works, step by step

1

DRHP / RHP

The company files a draft prospectus with the regulator. Investors and analysts read it for the business details, financials, and risks.

2

Price band announced

The company sets a floor and cap price. You can bid at any price in this range.

3

Bidding window

Over a few days, retail investors apply through their bank's UPI or a broker. Multiple shares can be bid by submitting more than one application.

4

Allotment & listing

After the offer closes, shares are allotted and the stock begins trading on pre-announced dates.

Key IPO terms you should know

Price Band

The floor and cap price per share set by the company. The final price is usually the upper end of the band.

Lot Size

The minimum number of shares you must apply for. One lot × the price is your minimum application amount.

GMP

Grey Market Premium — the unofficial price people are willing to pay for shares before listing. It is volatile, unofficial, and never a guarantee.

Subscription

How many times the offer was over-subscribed. 10x means applications for ten times the shares on offer.

Allotment

When oversubscribed, a lottery decides which applicants receive shares. Your money is refunded if you are not allotted.

Listing

The day the shares debut on the exchange. Listing price can differ from the issue price.

SME IPOs vs Mainboard IPOs

Feature Mainboard IPO SME IPO
Typical application size Usually ₹15,000 or below Usually above ₹15,000
Exchange segment NSE Mainboard / BSE Mainboard NSE Emerge / BSE SME
Company size Larger, more established companies Smaller and newer companies
Disclosure and liquidity Generally higher Generally lower
Risk profile Moderate Usually higher
